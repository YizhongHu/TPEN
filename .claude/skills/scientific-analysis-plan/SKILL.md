# Scientific analysis plan

The study owner writes this before the detailed runbook; the planner and
analyst read it to shape the experiment and its interpretation. Use prose
that helps them think, with headings only where useful.

Write the background like a thesis: link prior reports and their evidence,
connect the ideas and lead logically to the open questions. No expected
answer is required; expectations can mislead the later analyst.

Explain how the evidence can prove or disprove the hypothesis, under which
assumptions, and fix the analysis method beforehand. Discuss measurement,
population and uncertainty where they affect that argument. Make **what
this experiment cannot prove** prominent, including where the assumptions
cease to apply.

Consider potential outcomes outside that discussion and how to handle them.
Naming none is fine. “I have no plan; stop and rethink” is sufficient.
